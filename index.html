❌ #1: No Local Seattle/WA Context

Every page reads like it could be for any city. Add: King County permit requirements, Seattle-area building codes, common pipe types in Kent/Maple Valley homes, local landmarks, specific neighborhoods served. Google ranks pages that prove local knowledge.

❌ #2: No FAQ Sections or Schema

Zero FAQPage schema across the site. Every service page needs 4–5 questions targeting "How much does [service] cost in Kent WA?" These appear in AI Overviews, People Also Ask, and improve CTR by 15–25%.

❌ #3: No Cost Transparency

None of the service pages include pricing. High-intent users want cost ranges before calling. Add: "Sump pump repair in Kent WA typically costs $150–$600" to every page. Pages with cost information rank 2–3 positions higher on average.

⚠️ #4: Thin Service Area Pages

Bellevue (7,081 impr), Ballard (989 impr), Fremont, Greenwood, Magnolia pages are nearly empty. Each needs 600+ words: neighborhood history, common home ages/pipe types, local drainage issues, specific services offered in that area.

⚠️ #5: No Internal Link Structure

Service pages don't link to each other or to the blog. Hub pages (sewer-and-drain, trenchless, plumbing) need to properly link to all sub-pages. Every blog post should link to 2–3 relevant service pages.

💡 #6: No Reviews or Trust Signals

No customer quotes, no star rating display, no review count on any page. Adding Google Review snippets to service pages with AggregateRating schema is a proven CTR improvement tactic for local service businesses.
